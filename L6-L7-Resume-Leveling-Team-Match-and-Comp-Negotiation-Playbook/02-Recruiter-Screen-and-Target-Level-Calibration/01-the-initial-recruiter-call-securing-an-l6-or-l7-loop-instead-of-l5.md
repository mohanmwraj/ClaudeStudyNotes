**Integrity rule.** Asking for the right level is not inflation. It's giving the recruiter the evidence they need to set up the right loop. Every script here asks for a level your evidence supports and invites them to test it.

---

## 0. The 60-second version

1. **The first recruiter call sets the loop's level, and the loop sets the ceiling.** At many large companies, an L5 loop can't easily produce an L6 offer, because it never collects L6 signal ⚠. Fix the level before the loop is scheduled.
2. **Recruiters level on priors:** title, company, years, and the altitude of your first two minutes. You control only the last one, so spend it on scope.
3. **Make a 90-second level case:** current scope, two proofs of cross-team impact, the ask, and an invitation to test it.
4. **Ask four questions on every call:** what level the req is open at, who sets the target level, whether the loop composition matches L6, and what the intake note to the hiring manager will say.
5. **If they propose L5,** don't accept or refuse on the spot. Ask for the L6 loop, offer evidence, and offer a hiring-manager conversation. Decide later, against your alternatives.
6. **Never say "any level", "flexible on level" or "title doesn't matter".** Each one is an instruction to level you as low as the loop allows.
7. **Comp numbers wait.** The first call is about level and fit. Numbers come after the level is set (02.2).

## 1. What the recruiter controls, and what they don't

### 1.1 The recruiter's real levers

| Lever | Who holds it ⚠ | What you can influence |
|---|---|---|
| **The req's level band** (L5, L6, L5–L6, L6–L7) | Hiring manager and headcount plan; recruiter knows it | You can ask whether the req can be leveled up if the loop supports it |
| **The target level for your loop** | Recruiter proposes; HM or hiring lead approves | Your evidence, in the recruiter's intake note |
| **The loop's composition** (how many design rounds, a leadership round, a deep dive) | Follows the target level | Ask that it be the L6 composition |
| **The intake note to the hiring manager** | Recruiter | Give them the five-line level case (01.1 Script 10) |
| **The final level** | Loop feedback plus a committee, debrief or hiring lead | Only through the loop's signal |
| **Timeline and scheduling** | Recruiter and coordinators | A lot (02.2) |

The recruiter is measured on filling reqs with people who get through. A candidate pitched at L6 who fails costs them time; a candidate pitched at L5 who sails through costs them nothing. **Their default is to under-level.** Your evidence has to move them off the default.

### 1.2 How the target level shapes the loop ⚠

Loops at Staff and above usually carry more design and leadership weight than Senior loops: an extra system-design round, a behavioral round focused on cross-team influence, sometimes a past-project deep dive. A loop set up at L5 typically asks Senior-sized questions, and interviewers write feedback against the Senior rubric. Even a strong performance then produces "strong L5" feedback, and a committee rarely levels up past the signal it has.

That's why the first call matters more than its 30 minutes suggest. **You're not asking for a title. You're asking for a loop that can see Staff signal.**

### 1.3 Company patterns ⚠

These are commonly reported patterns, not published policy, and they change. Use them to choose your questions, then confirm with the recruiter.

| Company type | Who sets the target level | Can it move mid-process? | What usually differs at L6+ | Team match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Committee-based** (Google-style) | Recruiter with the hiring lead, before the loop | Up or down after the loop, through the committee | More design weight; leadership signal | After the committee ⚠ |
| **Debrief and bar-raiser** (Amazon-style) | The req is level-specific; the recruiter maps you to a req | A down-level to a lower req is common; up-leveling is rarer ⚠ | Leadership-principle depth; scope of examples | Usually hired into a specific team |
| **Candidate review + team selection** (Meta-style) | Recruiter, with a screen calibrated to the level | Down-leveling after review is reported ⚠ | An extra system-design round reported at E6 ⚠ | Reported before the offer since ~2023 ⚠ |
| **HM-driven team hiring** (NVIDIA-, Apple-, many product companies) | The hiring manager, who owns the req | Usually decided by the HM after the loop | Domain depth and fit for the team's roadmap | You interview for one team |
| **Indian unicorns and growth companies** | The HM or a hiring council | Often negotiable; titles vary widely | Ownership, speed, breadth | One team, often decided early |

Amazon describes its Bar Raisers as "objective third-party advisers" in the interview process, who take part in the debrief and, with the hiring manager, make the final hiring decision ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-bar-raiser)). That's the reason an Amazon loop's *level* is mostly fixed by the req you're mapped to: get the req right on the first call.

## 2. Why candidates get slotted at L5

### 2.1 The recruiter's priors

| Prior | How it pushes you down | What overrides it |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | "Lead" or "Senior" at a non-FAANG reads as L5 | The scope note and evidence (01.1 §7.3) |
| **Company tier** | Non-tech or services companies get discounted ⚠ | Numbers that show scale or complexity |
| **Years** | Years are a prior, not a rule ⚠; under ~10 often reads Senior | Evidence of cross-team scope regardless of years |
| **Resume altitude** | Executor bullets read L5 | 01.1's five-slot bullets |
| **Your first two minutes** | "I worked on…", "I was part of…" | A scope-first opening (Script 1) |
| **Your answer to "what level?"** | Any hedge becomes the level | A clear ask with evidence (§3) |

### 2.2 The "we'll decide after the loop" trap

Recruiters often say "Let's do the loop and the committee will decide the level." That's sometimes true. But the loop's *composition* is decided now. If it's built as a Senior loop, the committee will decide between "L5" and "no hire". Your reply (Script 3): "That works, as long as the loop can see L6 signal. Can we make sure it includes [two design rounds / a leadership round]?"

## 3. The 90-second level case

### 3.1 Structure

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | 15 s | Current role and scope in one sentence, scope-first |
| **Proof 1: breadth** | 25 s | Something that crossed team boundaries, with a number |
| **Proof 2: direction** | 25 s | Something you decided that others now follow |
| **The ask** | 15 s | The level, and the loop that can test it |
| **The invitation** | 10 s | "Does that match what the team needs?" |

### 3.2 Candidate V's version

> "I'm a Staff engineer at a marketplace with about 2,000 engineers. I'm the tech lead for config and rollout safety: the platform 1,200 services use. Two things I'd point to. First, I wrote the cross-org RFC that changed how every config change ships: staged rings and automatic halts. Config-caused Sev-1s went to zero. Second, at my previous company I drove a PCI program across 14 teams that cut our audit scope from 140 hosts to 12. So I'm looking at Staff-level roles, and I'd like this loop set up at L6 so it can test that. Does that match what the team needs?"

That's about 85 seconds spoken. It contains a level ask, three numbers, two cross-team proofs, and a question that hands the conversation back.

### 3.3 When to ask for L7

Ask for an L7 loop only when you can show **direction-setting beyond one org**, sustained over years: a company-wide standard, a multi-org strategy, an industry-visible artifact. V asks for L7 only at Loop A, where the req was already L7, and treats L6 as a plausible outcome. Asking for L7 without that evidence costs you: you may get an L7 loop you fail, when an L6 loop would have produced an offer.

## 4. The four questions for every first call

| # | Question | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What level is this req open at, and can it flex up if the loop supports it?" | Finds the band and whether there's headroom |
| 2 | "Who sets the target level for my loop, and when?" | Tells you who to convince and by when |
| 3 | "What does the L6 loop look like: how many design rounds, and is there a leadership or deep-dive round?" | Makes sure the loop can see Staff signal |
| 4 | "What will your note to the hiring manager say about me? Can I send you a five-line summary for it?" | Shapes the first thing the HM reads |

## 5. When the recruiter proposes L5

### 5.1 The options

| Option | When it fits | Script |
|---|---|---|
| **Push with evidence** | You have cross-team scope and numbers | Script 4 |
| **Ask for an L6 loop "with a down-level as a possible outcome"** | The recruiter is unsure, not opposed | Script 4 (second half) |
| **Ask for a hiring-manager conversation first** | The HM can see scope the recruiter can't | Script 5 |
| **Take the L5 loop deliberately** | You want the company more than the level, and you've decided that in advance | Script 7 |
| **Decline, warmly** | You have L6 alternatives | Script 6 |

### 5.2 Don't decide on the call

"Let me think about that and get back to you tomorrow" is always available. Use it. The worst outcome is an L5 loop you agreed to in the moment because you didn't want to seem difficult.

### 5.3 The cost of an L5 loop, honestly ⚠

- **Up-leveling past the loop's signal is rare,** but it happens when the feedback is strongly above bar in design and leadership.
- **The comp gap between L5 and L6** at large companies is substantial, and it compounds through refreshers and promotions (Folder 05 models it).
- **Promotion from L5 to L6 after joining** usually takes a year or more of demonstrated Staff scope ⚠, and it's not guaranteed.
- **But** an L5 offer at a company you strongly prefer can still be the right choice. The mistake is drifting into it, not choosing it.

## 6. India-specific realities

- **The first-two-minutes triad.** Many Indian recruiters, in-house and agency, open with "current CTC, expected CTC, notice period". Answer the notice period plainly; defer the two numbers until the level is set (02.2 Scripts 1–3).
- **Agency and RPO recruiters.** Third-party recruiters often submit you to a company's req with a fixed level band. Ask the agency which level the *company* has approved for your submission, and ask for the in-house recruiter's name once you're in process.
- **Notice period as a level signal.** It isn't one, but a 90-day notice period can make a recruiter deprioritize you. Mention whether a buyout or early release is possible, only if true.
- **Title inflation in Indian growth companies.** A "Principal Engineer" title at a 300-person company may map to L5 or L6 at a large company ⚠. When F offers "Principal", V asks what the scope is, not what the title is.

## 7. Before → After: recruiter-call answers

Each pair is a real moment on the first call. Weak answers are flagged by `script_lint.py`; Strong ones are clean (§9.3).

#### S1 · "What level are you targeting?"

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | Honestly I'm flexible on level, I just want to join a great company and grow. |
| **Strong** | Staff, L6. I'm the tech lead for a platform 1,200 services depend on, and I wrote the RFC that sets our config-safety policy across the org. I'd like the loop set up at L6 so it can test that. |

#### S2 · "We think this is a Senior role. Would that work?"

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | Sure, title doesn't matter to me, I'm happy at senior as long as the work is good. |
| **Strong** | I'd like to understand the scope first. If the role owns a system several teams depend on, I'd expect it to be L6, and I'd ask for the L6 loop so the panel can judge. Could we check the band with the hiring manager? |

#### S3 · "Tell me about yourself."

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | I'm just a backend engineer, I've worked on lots of things like Java, Kafka, some C++ and AI stuff. |
| **Strong** | I'm the Staff engineer for config and rollout safety at a 2,000-engineer marketplace. The thread through my career is making data move correctly and cheaply at every layer, from avionics firmware to a 48,000-instance platform. |

#### S4 · "Why are you looking?"

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | To be honest my manager is terrible and the place has become toxic. |
| **Strong** | The platform is in good shape and handed to a strong team. I'm looking for the next multi-year problem that crosses team boundaries, and this role's description reads like one. |

#### S5 · "What's your current CTC and what are you expecting?"

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | My current CTC is 1.4 crore and I'm expecting at least 2 crore. |
| **Strong** | I'd like to set the level first, because the comp follows from it. Once we know it's L6, I'm confident we'll find a number that works, and I'll share what I need then. |

#### S6 · "Are you interviewing elsewhere?"

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | No, not interviewing anywhere else, you're my only option right now. |
| **Strong** | Yes, I'm in process with a few companies for Staff roles, at different stages. I'll keep you updated so we can line up timelines. |

#### S7 · "Why this company?"

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | It's a great company, I've always wanted to work at a big brand like this. |
| **Strong** | Your team's work on rollout safety for edge devices is the problem I've spent my career on from both ends: firmware and fleet-scale platforms. |

#### S8 · "The committee will decide the level after the loop."

| Version | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | Okay, whatever level they decide is fine with me. |
| **Strong** | That works, as long as the loop can see L6 signal. Can we confirm it includes two design rounds and a leadership round, the way an L6 loop would? |

## 8. Handling the first touch in writing

- **InMail or email pitching a Senior role:** answer within a day, warmly, with the level redirect (01.1 Script 2).
- **A form asking for "expected CTC":** if it's mandatory, write "Open; to discuss after leveling" where free text is allowed. If a number is required, see 02.2 §3.
- **A recruiter who wants a call "to discuss your profile":** reply with two time slots and the five-line level case (01.1 Script 10), so the call starts from your framing.

## 9. The listing: `script_lint.py`

### 9.1 What it does

It flags phrases that cost Staff and Principal candidates level, leverage or credibility. The rules are grouped in **packs**:

| Pack | Used in | Rules beyond the universal set |
|---|---|---|
| (universal) | Every pack | APOLOGY, HEDGE, LEVEL-SURRENDER, NEEDY, BADMOUTH, SELF-DEPRECATE, ANXIETY, OVERSHARE |
| `recruiter` | Folder 02 | COMP-DISCLOSURE, EARLY-NUMBER, NO-ALTERNATIVES, GENERIC-INTEREST, SCOPE-SURRENDER, RANGE-GIVING |
| `hm` | Folder 03 | GENERIC-INTEREST, SCOPE-SURRENDER, OVERPROMISE, SOLUTIONING |
| `hc` | Folder 04 | PASSENGER, ARGUE-INTERVIEWER, DEMAND, ULTIMATUM |
| `comp` | Folder 05 | COMP-DISCLOSURE, NO-ALTERNATIVES, OVERPROMISE, DEMAND, ULTIMATUM, PREMATURE-YES, RANGE-GIVING, AUCTION, BLUFF |

To use it: write your answers one per line in `answers.txt` and run `python -I script_lint.py --pack recruiter answers.txt`. It checks wording only. A clean answer that isn't true for you is still a bad answer.

### 9.2 Listing

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""script_lint.py: flags the phrases that cost Staff/Principal candidates level, leverage or credibility
in recruiter, hiring-manager, committee and compensation conversations.

It checks wording, not truth. A clean script can still be a bad idea if it isn't true for you.

Usage:
  python -I script_lint.py --pack recruiter answers.txt   one answer per line
  python -I script_lint.py --kb FILE.md                   lint the Weak/Strong rows of a KB file;
                                                          the pack comes from its <!-- lint-pack: NAME --> marker
Packs: recruiter, hm, hc, comp. Universal rules apply in every pack.
Standard library only. Python 3.10+.
"""
import re
import sys

ALL = {"recruiter", "hm", "hc", "comp"}
RULES = [
    # name, packs, pattern, why it hurts
    ("APOLOGY", ALL, r"\b(?:sorry|apologi[sz]e|unfortunately|I know it looks|forgive me)\b",
     "apologizing frames a fact as a fault"),
    ("HEDGE", ALL, r"\b(?:I think maybe|maybe I could|kind of|sort of|I guess|hopefully|I'm not sure (?:if|but)"
     r"|just wanted to|if that's (?:ok|okay|alright)|I was wondering if|would it be possible to maybe)\b",
     "hedges read as low conviction"),
    ("LEVEL-SURRENDER", ALL, r"\b(?:any level|whatever level|open to any|flexible on (?:the )?level"
     r"|happy (?:with|at) (?:L5|E5|senior)|level doesn't matter|fine with (?:L5|E5|senior)"
     r"|don't care about (?:the )?(?:level|title)|title doesn't matter)\b",
     "invites the lowest level the loop can justify"),
    ("NEEDY", ALL, r"\b(?:really need this|desperate|dream job|I'll take anything|anything you can do"
     r"|whatever you can offer|grateful for any)\b", "signals you have no alternative"),
    ("BADMOUTH", ALL, r"\b(?:toxic|incompetent|idiots?|terrible manager|was terrible|terrible at|hate my|clueless)\b",
     "criticizing a past employer reads as the real reason you left"),
    ("SELF-DEPRECATE", ALL, r"\b(?:I'm just a|I was only|only a small part|not really an expert|I'm no expert"
     r"|I got lucky|I don't really know)\b", "undersells the scope you're asking them to level"),
    ("ANXIETY", ALL, r"\b(?:nervous|worried|scared|I hope (?:they're|it's) not)\b",
     "anxiety reads as doubt about the level you're asking for"),
    ("OVERSHARE", ALL, r"\b(?:to be honest|honestly|frankly|between you and me|off the record)\b",
     "implies the rest wasn't candid, or shares what shouldn't be"),
    ("COMP-DISCLOSURE", {"recruiter", "comp"}, r"\b(?:my (?:current )?(?:CTC|salary|comp(?:ensation)?|package|base)"
     r" is|I (?:currently )?(?:make|earn|am earning|draw)\b|current CTC of)",
     "volunteering current pay anchors the offer to your past, not the role"),
    ("EARLY-NUMBER", {"recruiter"}, r"(?:\b(?:I'm|I am) (?:expecting|looking for) (?:at least |around |about )?"
     r"(?:₹|\$|INR|USD)?\s?\d|\bmy expectation is\b|\bexpected CTC (?:is|of)\b"
     r"|\b\d+(?:\.\d+)?\s?(?:LPA|lakhs?|crores?)\b)",
     "a number before the level is set anchors the whole process"),
    ("NO-ALTERNATIVES", {"recruiter", "comp"}, r"\b(?:no other (?:offers?|interviews?|process(?:es)?)"
     r"|not interviewing (?:anywhere|elsewhere)|you(?:'re| are) my only|only (?:company|offer) I(?:'m| am))\b",
     "removes the recruiter's urgency"),
    ("GENERIC-INTEREST", {"recruiter", "hm"}, r"\b(?:great company|love the culture|big brand"
     r"|always wanted to work (?:at|for)|prestigious)\b", "praise without a problem you want to solve"),
    ("SCOPE-SURRENDER", {"recruiter", "hm"}, r"\b(?:whatever you need|any team is fine|happy (?:to do|with) "
     r"(?:anything|whatever)|whatever work)\b", "a Staff hire is chosen for a problem; 'anything' says you have none"),
    ("OVERPROMISE", {"hm", "comp"}, r"\b(?:I guarantee|guaranteed|I promise|100% (?:sure|certain))\b",
     "promising results you don't yet control is a credibility loan you'll have to repay"),
    ("SOLUTIONING", {"hm"}, r"\b(?:the first thing I(?:'d| would) do is (?:rewrite|replace|migrate|rebuild)"
     r"|I(?:'d| would) (?:rewrite|replace|rebuild)\b[^.]{0,40}\b(?:first|immediately|right away))",
     "prescribing before diagnosing is the classic outsider mistake"),
    ("PASSENGER", {"hc"}, r"\b(?:we decided|the team decided|I was asked to|I was told to|my manager asked me to"
     r"|management decided)\b", "a committee can't level a decision it can't attribute to you"),
    ("ARGUE-INTERVIEWER", {"hc"}, r"\b(?:the interviewer was (?:wrong|biased|unfair)|didn't understand my"
     r"|unfair|biased against|the question was (?:unfair|wrong))\b",
     "re-litigating feedback makes the committee defend its interviewer"),
    ("DEMAND", {"hc", "comp"}, r"\b(?:I demand|you must|you have to give me|I insist)\b",
     "demands invite a no; asks with reasons invite a yes"),
    ("ULTIMATUM", {"hc", "comp"}, r"\b(?:take it or leave it|or I walk|final offer or|I'll withdraw unless)\b",
     "an ultimatum you won't carry out destroys every later ask"),
    ("PREMATURE-YES", {"comp"}, r"\b(?:I accept|I'll take it|that works for me|sounds good,? let's do it)\b(?![^.]*\bif\b)",
     "an unconditional yes ends the negotiation"),
    ("RANGE-GIVING", {"recruiter", "comp"}, r"\b(?:somewhere (?:between|in the range)|anything (?:above|over)\b"
     r"|between (?:₹|\$|INR)?\s?\d[\d,.]*\s?(?:L|lakh|k|K|Cr)?\s?and\s?(?:₹|\$|INR)?\s?\d"
     r"|\d[\d,.]*\s?(?:L|lakhs?|k|K|Cr|crores?)?\s?(?:to|-|–)\s?(?:₹|\$|INR)?\s?\d[\d,.]*\s?"
     r"(?:L|lakhs?|k|K|Cr|crores?|LPA)\b)",
     "a range hands them its bottom"),
    ("AUCTION", {"comp"}, r"\b(?:beat whatever|outbid|bidding war|beat their (?:offer|counter)|top whatever)\b",
     "running an auction makes every party doubt you'll stay"),
    ("BLUFF", {"comp"}, r"\b(?:multiple offers|several offers|lots of offers|other companies are offering"
     r"|everyone is offering)\b", "vague, unverifiable leverage gets called"),
]
COMPILED = [(n, p, re.compile(rx, re.I), why) for n, p, rx, why in RULES]
ROW = re.compile(r"^\|\s*\*\*(Weak|Strong)\*\*\s*\|\s*(.+?)\s*\|\s*$")
PAIR = re.compile(r"^#{3,5}\s+(S\d+)\b")
PACK = re.compile(r"<!--\s*lint-pack:\s*(\w+)\s*-->")


def lint(text, pack):
    hits = []
    for name, packs, rx, why in COMPILED:
        if pack in packs:
            m = rx.search(text)
            if m:
                hits.append((name, m.group(0), why))
    return hits


def kb_mode(path):
    lines, fenced = [], False
    for ln in open(path, encoding="utf-8").read().split("\n"):
        if ln.lstrip().startswith("```"):
            fenced = not fenced
        if not fenced:
            lines.append(ln)
    pack = next((m.group(1) for ln in lines for m in [PACK.search(ln)] if m), None)
    pair, seen, bad = "?", {"Weak": [0, 0], "Strong": [0, 0]}, []
    if pack is None:
        print("no lint-pack marker; 0 pairs")
    for ln in lines:
        m = PAIR.match(ln)
        if m:
            pair = m.group(1)
            continue
        m = ROW.match(ln)
        if not m or pack is None:
            continue
        kind, text = m.group(1), m.group(2)
        hits = lint(text, pack)
        ok = bool(hits) if kind == "Weak" else not hits
        seen[kind][0] += 1
        seen[kind][1] += ok
        names = ",".join(h[0] for h in hits) or "clean"
        print(f"{'ok ' if ok else 'BAD'} {pair:<5} {kind:<6} {names}")
        if not ok:
            bad.append(f"{pair} {kind}")
    print("-" * 60)
    print(f"pack: {pack}")
    print("Weak rows flagged: %d/%d" % (seen["Weak"][1], seen["Weak"][0]))
    print("Strong rows clean: %d/%d" % (seen["Strong"][1], seen["Strong"][0]))
    print("VERDICT: " + ("OK" if not bad else "FAIL " + "; ".join(bad)))
    return 0 if not bad else 1


def file_mode(path, pack):
    n = flagged = 0
    for ln in open(path, encoding="utf-8"):
        ln = ln.strip()
        if not ln:
            continue
        n += 1
        hits = lint(ln, pack)
        flagged += bool(hits)
        print(("FLAG " if hits else "ok   ") + (ln if len(ln) <= 70 else ln[:67] + "..."))
        for name, frag, why in hits:
            print(f"     {name}: \"{frag}\" ({why})")
    print(f"{flagged}/{n} answers flagged (pack: {pack})")
    return 0


def main(argv):
    sys.stdout.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8")
    if len(argv) == 3 and argv[1] == "--kb":
        return kb_mode(argv[2])
    if len(argv) == 4 and argv[1] == "--pack" and argv[2] in ALL:
        return file_mode(argv[3], argv[2])
    print(__doc__)
    return 2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main(sys.argv))
```

## 10. Word-for-word scripts

#### Script 1 · Verbal: the opening two minutes of the call

```text
"Thanks for setting this up. Quick context so you can place me: I'm a Staff engineer at
[marketplace], about 2,000 engineers. I'm the tech lead for config and rollout safety, the
platform 1,200 services use. Before that I led checkout payments and a PCI program across
14 teams at [retailer], and I started in avionics firmware and low-latency C++.

I'm looking at Staff-level roles. Before I go deeper, can you tell me what the team is
trying to solve and what level the req is open at? That'll help me focus on what's relevant."
```

#### Script 2 · Verbal: the 90-second level case (§3.2)

```text
"Two things I'd point to. First, I wrote the cross-org RFC that changed how every config
change ships at [marketplace]: staged rings and automatic halts. Config-caused Sev-1s went
to zero. Second, at [retailer] I drove a PCI program across 14 teams that cut our audit
scope from 140 hosts to 12.

So I'm looking at Staff-level roles, and I'd like this loop set up at L6 so it can test
that. Does that match what the team needs?"
```

#### Script 3 · Verbal: "Let's do the loop and the committee will decide the level"

```text
"That works for me, as long as the loop can see L6 signal. Could we confirm the loop has the
L6 shape: two system-design rounds and a round on leadership or cross-team influence? If the
panel only sees Senior-sized questions, the committee can only choose between Senior and
no-hire, and that wouldn't be a fair test for either of us."
```

#### Script 4 · Verbal: the recruiter proposes L5

```text
"Thanks for being direct. Let me give you two data points and then a suggestion.

The platform I lead serves 1,200 services, and the policy I wrote applies to about 2,000
engineers. The PCI program I drove changed how 14 teams deploy. That's the scope I'd bring.

My suggestion: set the loop up at L6. If the panel doesn't see Staff signal, a Senior
outcome is possible and I understand that. But if we start at L5, the panel can't see the
scope at all. Would the hiring manager be open to that?"
```

## 11. Probe chains

<details><summary><b>P1 · "Why do you think you're Staff?"</b></summary>

**Probe:** "Your title is Staff, but titles vary. What makes you Staff by our standard?"

**What they're testing:** whether you can describe scope in their terms, without leaning on your title.

**Executor answer:** "I've been doing this for 14 years and I'm the most senior engineer on my team."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "Three things, by most companies' Staff definitions: the system I lead is used by 1,200 services, so my decisions land on dozens of teams; I set direction that others follow without me in the room, through the config-safety RFC; and I've driven programs across teams I don't manage, the PCI work across 14 teams. I'd expect your loop to test exactly those."

</details>

<details><summary><b>P2 · "Your years are a bit light for Staff."</b></summary>

**Probe:** (To a candidate with nine years) "Most of our Staff hires have 12+ years."

**What they're testing:** whether you'll back down on a prior that isn't a rule.

**Executor answer:** "Oh, okay. Maybe Senior makes more sense then."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "Understood that it's the usual range. What I'd ask the panel to judge is scope, not years: [two proofs]. If the loop shows Senior scope, I'll accept that outcome. I'd just like it to be the loop that decides."

</details>

<details><summary><b>P3 · "The hiring manager wants someone hands-on."</b></summary>

**Probe:** "This team needs someone who codes every day. Staff people here sometimes just write documents."

**What they're testing:** whether "Staff" means you've stopped building.

**Executor answer:** "I can do whatever coding is needed, I'm very flexible."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "Good. I still write production code: I wrote the Java reference SDK and co-wrote the rollout controller on the config platform. Staff for me means my code and my designs both affect other teams. I'd expect the coding rounds to check that I'm current."

</details>

<details><summary><b>P4 · "We only have a Senior req right now."</b></summary>

**Probe:** "The only open req on this team is Senior."

**What they're testing:** often nothing: it's a constraint. It's also a test of whether you'll take the first thing offered.

**Executor answer:** "That's fine, I'll interview for it."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "Thanks for telling me. Is there a Staff req on a neighboring team, or is this team expecting a Staff req later this year? If not, I'd rather wait for the right one than take a loop that can't see what I'd bring. Please keep me in mind."

</details>

<details><summary><b>P5 · "You're asking for L7. What makes you Principal?"</b></summary>

**Probe:** (Loop A) "The req is Principal, but most candidates we see for it are at your level and get offered L6."

**What they're testing:** whether you understand the L7 bar and the honest odds.

**Executor answer:** "I'm confident I'm a Principal."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "My strongest L7 evidence is the config-safety policy: it's company-wide, it changed how every team ships, and it's held for two years. I also know the bar is direction across orgs, sustained, and I have one strong example of that rather than several. So I'd like to interview against the L7 req, and I understand L6 is a real possible outcome. Is the L6 req on the same team open too?"

</details>

<details><summary><b>P6 · "What's your expected CTC? I need it for the form."</b></summary>

**Probe:** "I can't move forward without an expected number."

**What they're testing:** often, simply a process requirement. Sometimes, an early anchor.

**Executor answer:** "Okay, I'm expecting 2 crore."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "I understand it's needed. Can you put 'open, to discuss after leveling'? If the field needs a number, I'll give you one once we've confirmed L6, because the right number depends on the level. Or if you can share the band for L6, I'll tell you whether we're in the same range." (02.2 covers the full sequence.)

</details>

<details><summary><b>P7 · "Why should I put you up as Staff rather than Senior?"</b></summary>

**Probe:** "If I pitch you as Staff and you're down-leveled, that's on me."

**What they're testing:** the recruiter's own risk. They need ammunition, not confidence.

**Executor answer:** "You won't regret it, I promise."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "Fair. Here's what you can put in the note: the 1,200-service platform, the org-wide RFC, the 14-team PCI program, with numbers. And if it helps, I'm glad to do a short call with the hiring manager first, so the level decision is theirs as well as yours."

</details>

<details><summary><b>P8 · "We'll start at Senior and up-level if you do well."</b></summary>

**Probe:** "Don't worry, if you crush it, we'll up-level you."

**What they're testing:** whether you know how leveling works.

**Executor answer:** "Great, that sounds fair."

**Staff/Principal answer:** "I appreciate that. My worry is practical: if the questions are Senior-sized, there's less room to show Staff signal, however well I do. Could we keep at least the design rounds at the L6 bar? Then up-leveling has real evidence behind it."

</details>
